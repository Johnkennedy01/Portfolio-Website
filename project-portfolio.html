Mixer Microphone Extension

In 2018, I got a Tech contract to design and produce a Mixer Mic Extension for my client (a pastor) for Church use.

HOW IT WORKS: A microphone converts sound waves into a very small electrical signal. That signal is usually weak and needs to be amplified before it can be processed by a mixer.
A microphone extension system makes sure this weak signal arrives at the mixer cleanly, with minimal noise, distortion, or signal loss.

The Extension Involves

A long standard cable wire from mic to extension and to the mixer, A microphone preamp, Power Toggle Switch, 3 Jack plugs (Mono plugs) connector and socket outputs at the top, 1 Jack plug connector and socket input, at the back, 3 Volume Controls (Variable Resistor), 3 pin Male & Female Cannon socket and plug connector at the top.

Side View

This is a side view of the Mixer Mic Extension, showcasing its design and layout.

At the top of the extension, you can see the three output jack plug socket, three volume controls and the two cannon female output socket for mic cable wire connection.

Practical Design Rules

For reliable microphone extension:

  • Use balanced XLR cables whenever possible.
  • Keep audio cables away from power cables.
  • Use quality shielded cables.
  • Match the microphone type to the mixer input.
  • Apply phantom power only when required.
  • Avoid unnecessary long cable runs.
  • Use proper grounding and avoid loop problems.
  • Set gain carefully to prevent clipping.

Back View

The back view of the Mixer Mic Extension, shows its design and layout. It is where the input jack plug and socket connection is located at the back.

Working Theory

1. Sound to electrical conversion: A microphone diaphragm moves when sound waves hit it.
The movement changes an electrical property in the microphone element.
The result is a tiny analog voltage signal.

2. Signal strengthening: The signal is too weak for direct use.
The mixer input preamp increases the signal level for proper processing.

3. Noise-resistant transmission: The microphone signal must travel without picking up hum, interference, or loss.
Balanced cables and proper grounding help keep the signal clean.

Balanced vs Unbalanced Connection

This is one of the most important technical ideas.

Balanced connection:
  • Uses two signal wires plus a shield.
  • Helps cancel hum and electromagnetic interference.
  • Preferred for microphone extension.
  • Common in XLR and TRS cables.
Unbalanced connection
  • Uses one signal wire and a ground
  • More vulnerable to noise
  • Suitable only for short distances

Mixer Audio Amplifier

In 2016, I designed, fabricated and built a Mixer Audio Amplifier for a Client.

Basic Working Principle

The signal flow is generally:

  1. Audio Input: Sound enters from microphones, instruments, or media devices.
  2. Pre-Amplifier (Preamp): Boosts weak signals (especially microphone signals) to a usable level.
  3. Mixer Section: Combines multiple input channels and allows level adjustment.
  4. Master Volume Control: Sets the overall output level.
  5. Power Amplifier: Increases the signal power.
  6. Speaker Output: Sends the amplified signal to connected speakers.

Back View

The back view of the Mixer Audio Amplifier shows the input speaker terminals. Is where speakers can be connected to use.

Mixer Audio Amplifier: Is an electronic device that combines the functions of an audio mixer and a power amplifier in one unit. It allows multiple audio sources to be connected, mixed together, adjusted, and then amplified to drive loudspeakers.

Safety Precautions

  • Turn the volume down before switching the unit on or off.
  • Use speakers with the correct impedance and power rating.
  • Keep liquids away from the equipment.
  • Ensure adequate ventilation.
  • Disconnect power before servicing.
  • Use properly grounded AC outlets.

Front View

The front view of the Mixer Audio Amplifier shows the control panel, Transformer, XLR microphone inputs, Mixer volume controls, Audio output, Power switch, Master volume control.

Speaker Impedance

Typical supported speaker loads are:

  • 4Ω
  • 8Ω
  • 16Ω
